**severe  
**very strong and dangerous

**disruption  
**change or a pause which stops things happening normally

**flooding  
**where water spreads to land that is usually dry

**landslide  
**where earth, rocks, etc move quickly downhill

**mudslide  
**a type of landslide where liquid mud moves quickly downhill

**sea surface temperature  
**the temperature of the top layer of sea water

**gather strength  
**build up strength

**pick up  
**get stronger

**atmosphere  
**the air and gases surrounding the Earth

**impact  
**the result of something/have a strong effect on something

**storm surge  
**a sudden rise in sea level that moves inland

**widespread  
**affecting a large area

**casualty  
**deaths or injuries caused by a negative event

**TRANSCRIPT**

Note: This is not a word-for-word transcript.

**Phil  
**Climate change is affecting weather around the world. Is it changing hurricanes, cyclones and typhoons? Here's BBC weather forecaster and presenter, Darren Bett.

**Darren Bett  
**Climate change is **impacting** many things. It's making **severe** weather happen more often and it's making **severe** weather more **severe**. And we're likely to see changes with storms as well. Now, we don't expect that there will be more storms, but we expect that more of the storms will be stronger, more powerful, with stronger winds potentially causing much more damage and **disruption**.

Climate scientists also think that storms are slowing down, they're not moving across as quickly, and that means if a storm is in the same area for longer the winds will be stronger for longer, and the rain will last for longer producing more **flooding** and more likelihood of **flooding** and **landslides** and also **mudslides**.

The reason we think storms are likely to be stronger in the future is because of the **sea surface temperature**. Now remember a hurricane, a typhoon, a cyclone **gathers strength** from warm waters and we know our waters are becoming warmer and if a storm can move over a larger area of water with **sea surface temperatures** over 27 degrees, it's got more opportunity to strengthen and the winds to really **pick up**.

Another element of climate change, not just how our waters are warming, but how our **atmosphere** is warming and that means the rain that does fall is likely to be heavier producing much greater **impact**. And as if that wasn't enough, **storm surge**, that's that body of water that gets swept inland and produces **widespread** **flooding**. Climate change is warming our seas, and the sea levels are rising. That potentially could mean that it's a **storm surge** which is one element of a storm that produces more **casualties** than any other, those **storm surges** could become even more dangerous.

So, storms like hurricanes, typhoons and cyclones already **impact** so many people's lives and it could be that they're becoming more powerful in the years ahead.
